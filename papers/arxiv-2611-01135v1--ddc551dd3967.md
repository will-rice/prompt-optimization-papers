# Compile as Hypothesis: A Headroom Typology for Prompt Optimization on Real Reasoning Models

Zhengshuai Yang    Changyun Li ^(†)^(†)thanks: Corresponding author.

###### Abstract

Automated prompt compilers search for or rewrite prompts under the
implicit assumption that optimization consistently improves downstream
task performance. We evaluate this assumption on frontier reasoning
models through rigorous paired, same-day measurements. Across four
optimizer families (genome search, profile-guided mutation, GEPA, and
ESPO) and eight tasks, structured search yields no statistically
significant accuracy gains on strong reasoners. However, a discrete
structural genome provides essential architectural stability, preventing
catastrophic bimodal seed-collapse (where unconstrained search collapses
toward the judge floor in 3/5 runs). Through 190-problem evaluations on
knowledge-intensive benchmarks, we establish a three-dimensional
headroom typology: prompt optimization cannot compensate for intrinsic
knowledge deficits or physical token truncation, but successfully
resolves protocol and formatting errors. On gpt-6, while free-text
reflective search (GEPA) yields a $`+`$.14 gain on a task where
structured search remains flat, qualitative ablation reveals that
$`\sim`$.035 stems from judge-rubric reconstruction while $`\sim`$.105
reflects genuine instruction refinement. Our pre-registered
AutoAPC-Select deployment gate matches the oracle policy in 5/7 groups
and rescues seed-collapse arms with zero regret. Finally, we expose
silent backend-mixing in commercial APIs during outage windows and
provide automated per-call audit verification tools to ensure empirical
evaluation fidelity.

## 1 Introduction

The prompt required for a complex downstream task depends intimately on
the underlying LLM: models vary substantially in format sensitivity
(Sclar et al., 2024) and functional skill profiles (Chen et al., 2023;
Liang et al., 2023). While benchmark suites rank aggregate model
performance, they offer no actionable guidance for prompt construction.
Prompt compilers such as DSPy (Khattab et al., 2024) systematically
optimize prompts for specific models, but typically treat the benefit of
compilation as an implicit given. Without rigorous paired evaluation
against the uncompiled prompt, automated pipelines risk deploying
degraded or destabilized prompts without detecting the loss.

#### Research gap.

Existing prompt compilers (Khattab et al., 2024; Agrawal et al., 2025)
implicitly assume that optimization improves downstream performance and
deploy the best-scoring candidate without paired comparison to the
uncompiled base. Without direct measurement of the compilation prior,
automated pipelines risk deploying degraded prompts—especially when
search exhibits seed-level fragility or when the task lacks exploitable
headroom. No prior work systematically evaluates _when_ prompt
optimization succeeds versus when it fails, nor establishes operational
boundaries for deployment decisions.

We measure the compilation prior directly. Under our
_Compile-as-Hypothesis_ protocol, every compiler output is treated as a
testable hypothesis that must demonstrate empirical superiority before
deployment. The pipeline executes two roots in parallel under identical
evaluation budgets: a profile-rule root (apc-full) and the uncompiled
base root (apc-safe), deploying whichever arm proves superior on holdout
validation. The compilation prior, defined as
$`\Delta(\text{rule-root},\text{base-root})`$, is thus an empirically
signed quantity.

We evaluate this protocol across three complementary environments:
APCBench (a deterministic four-task simulator providing exact ground
truth across three distinct model profiles), real-world reasoning
experiments with qwen3.8-flash on three enterprise tasks, and five
external benchmarks unseen by the authors: GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021),
MATH Level-5 (Hendrycks et al., 2021), AIME 2024+25, GPQA-Diamond (Rein
et al., 2024), and HLE-exact (Phan et al., 2025). Our evaluation
benchmarks official reproductions of leading open-source optimizers,
including GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025) and ESPO (Anonymous, 2026b),
replicated across gpt-6 and independent infrastructure providers with
strict per-call backend verification.

Our empirical findings reveal fundamental boundary conditions for
automated prompt optimization: on frontier reasoning models, structured
prompt search yields no statistically significant accuracy gains. The
base-root arm reliably preserves zero-shot performance within the
empirical noise band across all real tasks, whereas unguided
profile-rule search introduces severe instability across seeds,
including bimodal collapse down to $`.13`$. Official GEPA and ESPO
reproductions similarly fall within the paired same-day noise band.
Under restricted token budgets, all six optimization pathways pin at the
judge floor. External competitive benchmarks reproduce these null gains,
as the model saturates benchmark ceilings (AIME: 60/60). Crucially, the
primary benefits of compilation are structural: a discrete genome
prevents catastrophic seed-collapse, and zero-shot cross-task transfer
matches target-task zero-shot performance without adaptation budget (up
to $`+`$.79 over cold re-search). Our pre-registered AutoAPC-Select
deployment gate successfully rescues both seed-collapse arms with zero
regret.

These findings demonstrate that the underlying _headroom dimension_
dictates whether prompt optimization can succeed. Our headroom typology
distinguishes three fundamental operational failure modes:

1.  1.  Knowledge headroom: Missing capabilities or domain facts in model
        weights.
2.  2.  Physical headroom: Truncated context or output token capacity.
3.  3.  Protocol headroom: Formatting, interface schema, and instruction
        parsing errors.

Prompt optimization successfully addresses protocol headroom (3) through
structured genomes, but fundamentally cannot resolve knowledge (1) or
physical (2) limits (Figure 1). In a 190-problem HLE-exact evaluation
with reachable headroom, all six optimization arms tie. The tie recurs
on GPQA-Diamond ($`n{=}190`$), where reflective search exhausts 28
iterations learning domain heuristics without closing the knowledge gap.
When tasks are knowledge-bound, pipelines must pivot to retrieval or
model scaling rather than wasting optimization cycles.

Furthermore, optimization dynamics depend heavily on search space
constraints. On gpt-6, free-text reflective search (GEPA) achieves a
$`+`$.14 gain on financial analysis where structured search shows zero
headroom. Analysis of the evolved prompt reveals that the optimizer
partially reconstructed the deterministic scoring rubric from failure
feedback; this judge-gaming accounts for $`\sim`$.035 of the score,
while $`\sim`$.105 represents genuine task-instruction refinement. Gated
ESPO remains null on the identical cell under verified backend
enforcement, showing that this divergence is unique to unconstrained
reflective rewriting. Finally, our multi-provider audit exposes an
undocumented threat to NLP benchmarking: during API outage windows,
commercial gateways silently reroute traffic to fallback models. We
implement an automated per-call audit mechanism that detects and
quarantines contaminated serving windows.

#### Why this negative result matters.

Our findings challenge the implicit assumption underlying automated
prompt engineering pipelines: that optimization consistently yields
deployable improvements. By demonstrating null gains across four
optimizer families on frontier reasoners, we establish clear operational
boundaries for when to invest in prompt search versus when to pivot to
model scaling or retrieval. The headroom typology prevents wasted
optimization cycles on knowledge-bound tasks, while AutoAPC-Select
safeguards against seed-level collapse observed in 3/5 unconstrained
runs. Without paired measurement, existing pipelines would blindly
deploy these degraded prompts.

## 2 Related work

#### Prompt optimization.

benefit of compilation implicit and uncompared against the uncompiled

#### Prompt sensitivity and evaluation reliability.

Sclar et al. (2024) quantify format sensitivity, and Wu et al. (2024)
show that evaluators reward style over substance. These findings
motivate our hardened exact-match judge and paired, same-day design.
Reasoning models exhibit cross-day score drift even at temp$`=0`$, so
all comparisons in this paper use paired measurements from the same day
and a quantified noise band.

#### Test-time compute.

Snell et al. (2024) study optimal test-time budget allocation for
reasoning. We consider the corresponding budget for prompt optimization.
We count task rollouts and exclude optimizer meta-calls, following
GEPA’s accounting so that the budgets are comparable.

#### Benchmarks.

GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021), MMLU-style
MCQ constraints, GPQA-Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), HLE-exact (Phan
et al., 2025), and ranking suites such as HELM (Liang et al., 2023) and
FLASK (Chen et al., 2023) provide context for model performance. Their
rankings do not determine whether a compiled prompt should be deployed.

## 3 Method

prior to prompt optimization: if the missing capability is intrinsic

### 3.1 Structural genome and task specification

A PromptGenome represents a prompt through discrete components: role,
constraints, reasoning directives, few-shot example blocks, output
directives (schema strictness and whether to include the schema in the
prompt), and verification hooks. It can be recompiled. A TaskSpec
defines the task contract, including the input mapping, gold schema, and
per-dimension scoring weights. Unlike continuous soft prompts, the
discrete genome is model-agnostic: it works with any renderer and can
transfer across models zero-shot (§5.3).

### 3.2 Two-root compilation

The compiler (apc-full) applies rules from the model profile to the base
genome. These capability-gated priors set individual loci: for example,
they add few-shot blocks only when the profile’s few-shot benefit is low
and strengthen schema directives when JSON reliability is weak. The base
root (apc-safe) is the uncompiled genome. Both roots run in parallel at
equal budget on the development set, and the winner is deployed. The
compilation prior $`=\Delta(\text{rule-root},\text{base-root})`$ is
therefore a measured, signed quantity (Algorithm 1).

0:  Base genome $`G_{0}`$, Profile $`P`$, Task $`T`$, Budget $`B`$

1:  $`G_{\text{rule}}\leftarrow\textsc{ApplyProfileRules}(G_{0},P)`$
{Rule root}

2:  $`G_{\text{safe}}\leftarrow G_{0}`$ {Base root}

3:
 $`G_{\text{rule}}^{*}\leftarrow\textsc{Evolve}(G_{\text{rule}},T,\text{budget}=B/2)`$

4:
 $`G_{\text{safe}}^{*}\leftarrow\textsc{Evolve}(G_{\text{safe}},T,\text{budget}=B/2)`$

5:
 $`S_{\text{rule}}\leftarrow\textsc{Evaluate}(G_{\text{rule}}^{*},\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})`$

6:
 $`S_{\text{safe}}\leftarrow\textsc{Evaluate}(G_{\text{safe}}^{*},\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})`$

7:  $`\Delta_{\text{prior}}\leftarrow S_{\text{rule}}-S_{\text{safe}}`$
{Signed compilation prior}

8:
 $`G_{\text{deploy}}\leftarrow\textsc{AutoAPC-Select}(G_{\text{rule}}^{*},G_{\text{safe}}^{*},S_{\text{rule}},S_{\text{safe}})`$

9:  return $`G_{\text{deploy}},\Delta_{\text{prior}}`$

### 3.3 Search

Evolution runs for two generations with a population of 8, an elite set
of 3, and a seeded random number generator. The GenomeMutator uniformly
selects a locus and an alternative value from the genome’s search space.
The ProfileGuidedMutator (PGAM) instead uses roulette sampling with
profile-prior weights and an elite-reinforced bandit correction.
Successive halving is available for large search spaces. We count task
rollouts (development examples scored) toward the budget and exclude
optimizer meta-calls, matching GEPA’s accounting.

### 3.4 Judge

A hardened exact-match judge (v3.2) supplies ground truth. It normalizes
answers, compares multisets to recognize equivalent notation variants,
and checks adversarial formats. Its regression suite contains 27
positive and 4 negative cases; we retain full prediction traces so that
scores can be recomputed. Human review of 12 failure samples led to
three judge revisions. We quantify judge reliability in §7.

### 3.5 Simulator

APCBench is a deterministic simulator covering four tasks (financial
analysis, contract extraction, math reasoning, and constraint following)
across three model profiles. It provides exact ground truth for
statistically powerful paired comparisons without API noise. All
simulation seeds are fixed. Analyses are CI-ordered: adding an analysis
does not change existing confidence intervals.

## 4 Experimental setup

#### Real APIs.

We use qwen3.8-flash through DashScope as the primary model, with a
whitelisted credential, and gpt-6 through an OpenAI-compatible gateway.
All runs use temp$`=0`$ with model-side reasoning enabled.

#### Protocol.

Unless stated otherwise, we use 5 development, 8 validation, and 20
holdout examples, a search budget of 8, and seed 42. Optimization cost
is measured strictly in _task rollouts_ (number of downstream model
calls evaluated) rather than dollar cost or token count, establishing an
invariant Pareto efficiency currency across fluctuating API pricing and
tokenization schemes. Every comparison is paired within the same day.
Repeated z0 evaluations yielded a same-day noise band of $`\pm`$.007 and
cross-day drift of .028. We treat gains within $`\pm`$.01 as noise.

#### Arms.

We compare zero-shot (base genome, no compilation), manual (hand-written
prompt), apc-full (rule-root search), and apc-safe (base-root search).
The baselines are GEPA and ESPO. We reproduce GEPA’s official Algorithm
1, with failure-driven reflective rewriting, per-instance Pareto parent
sampling, and champion selection on the full validation set. ESPO uses
Diagnose/Propose/bootstrap-Select.

#### External benchmarks.

We evaluate on GSM8K, MATH Level-5, AIME 2024+25 (60 problems),
GPQA-Diamond (190 holdout problems), and a stratified 90-problem
HLE-exact subset selected for reachable knowledge-type headroom
(zero-shot accuracy $`\approx`$.10).

#### Contamination control.

In the multi-provider experiments, we record every API response’s model
field in a per-call audit stream. A mismatch with the configured
expect_backend raises a retryable error. We exclude cells whose backend
cannot be verified from the paper’s claims (§6.3).

### 5.1 Simulator: gains depend on task structure

performs worse on single-locus tasks ($`-`$.013) because of selection
noise from successive halving. PGAM is indistinguishable from uniform
mutation (pooled $`+`$.0004). Migration reaches native quality at 30% of
the re-search budget.

### 5.2 Real qwen3.8-flash: the base root guarantees zero-shot stability

Across all three enterprise tasks (Table 2), the base-root arm reliably
preserves zero-shot performance within the empirical noise band
($`\pm.007`$, maximum deviation $`\leq.004`$). In contrast, the
unconstrained profile-rule root initiates search with substantial
initial deficits (0.2 to 0.53) and fails to fully recover within budget.
A five-seed replication reveals severe bimodal instability across tasks:
across seeds 43 to 47, apc-full yields scores of
$`.5704/.5705/.1334/.1318/.5639/.1428`$ on financial (mean .3085, SD
.2362; 3/5 runs collapsing toward the judge floor),
$`.9782/.9583/.1741`$ on contract (mean .7035, SD .4586), and
$`.8442/.8440/.6425`$ on math.

Conversely, apc-safe exhibits 100% stability, completely avoiding
collapse across all 12/12 evaluation runs. Its holdout scores
consistently track the zero-shot baseline with minimal variance (SD
$`\leq`$.005): financial .6664$`\pm`$.0047, contract .9771$`\pm`$.0003,
and math .8436$`\pm`$.0001. The two-root protocol thus functions as an
essential safeguard against unconstrained optimization collapse.

### 5.4 Comparison with strong baselines

To verify whether the null result is specific to APC’s discrete genome
search space, we evaluate against two leading open-source prompt
optimizers under identical rollouts and evaluation paths. The official
GEPA reproduction falls within the paired same-day noise band on
financial ($`+`$.0024), as does ESPO (.7009). Across all four
independent optimizer families—structured genome search, profile-guided
mutation (PGAM), reflective rewriting (GEPA), and cluster-and-stabilize
selection (ESPO)—accuracy gains on the strong reasoning model are
statistically indistinguishable from zero. This confirms that the lack
of headroom is a general property of the “strong reasoning model $`+`$
high judge-coverage” regime rather than a limitation of APC’s
parameterization.

### 5.5 Distinguishing headroom types

Three real-LLM experiments test whether prompt optimization can address
different types of headroom (Figure 1).

#### F10: restricted token budget (pre-registered).

With max_tokens set to 150, the model’s score falls to $`.15`$. All six
optimization pathways, including structured, reflective, and free-text
search, score at the judge floor on the same day. Prompt optimization
cannot compensate for physically truncated output capacity or supply
capabilities absent from the model’s weights.

#### F11: reachable knowledge-type headroom.

On a 90-problem HLE-exact subset with headroom (zero-shot accuracy .10,
ceiling .3+), all six arms tie (scores .388 to .412, with 2 or 3 correct
out of 30). A confirmatory $`n{=}100`$ round reproduces the tie (GEPA vs
zero-shot McNemar $`p{=}.219`$) and puts the same-text nondeterminism
floor at 2.7%. GEPA’s reflection learned domain rules, including SMILES
conventions and perturbation-theory regime qualifiers, but these
produced no improvement on the holdout.

#### GPQA-Diamond ($`n{=}190`$).

The tie recurs on a second real dataset. Five arms score .853 to .868,
and reflective search accepts zero candidates in 28 iterations
($`p{=}.219`$ class evidence). Re-evaluating the champion on holdout
gives .8856, within same-text repeatability ($`+`$.0127) of zero-shot
.8729. For knowledge-type headroom, these results support changing the
model instead of optimizing the prompt.

## 6 Analysis

### 6.1 Free-text search gains differ across models

GEPA yields a same-day null result on financial with qwen ($`+`$.0024).
On gpt-6, the same protocol gives a holdout score of .8914 against the
paired, same-path z0 score of .7039. The $`+`$.14 gain occurs on the
task where structured search showed zero headroom. The evolved prompt’s
style-rules section encodes the judge’s rubric: a two-template summary
constraint copied from the scoring dimensions, rules requiring metrics,
and the wording “violations score zero.” The reflective optimizer
reconstructed these rules from per-dimension failure traces. Removing
that section changes the score by $`-`$.035 (.8441 verbatim $`\to`$
.8092 stripped). The remaining $`\sim`$+.105 comes from task-instruction
rewriting, which raises judge-accuracy from .41 to between .68 and .75.

This result limits the no-headroom conclusion to a particular search
space. Free-text reflective rewriting finds gains that structured
mutation does not, and the difference depends on the model (null on
qwen, $`+`$.14 on gpt-6). The prompt also shows that free-text
optimizers without structural safeguards can game rule judges, although
gaming accounts for a minority of the measured gain here. The two-root
paired protocol exposed this behavior; unconditional deployment would
have accepted a partially gamed prompt. Gated ESPO, re-run on the same
cell under full backend enforcement, adopts no candidate and remains
null (.7021 vs .7039). The divergence is therefore specific to
unconditional best-of-search.

### 6.2 PGAM’s prior fails to transfer

We evaluated whether profile-guided mutation (PGAM) provides advantages
on frontier models by testing gpt-6 on math. Cold search with PGAM
scores .5960, compared with .6336 for uniform mutation ($`-`$.0376,
outside the noise band). The gpt-6 profile assigns a few-shot benefit of
0.0, which paradoxically leads the prior to weight exemplar loci
3$`\times`$ higher. Because frontier reasoners already possess strong
internal reasoning patterns, injecting few-shot blocks degrades
performance. Two generations of bandit correction cannot overcome this
misspecified prior within a budget of 8. This demonstrates that
heuristic defect-compensation priors derived from weaker or synthetic
models do not generalize to frontier reasoners; instead, they over-index
on redundant prompt components that impair reasoning flow.

### 6.3 Evaluation infrastructure: backend mixing and service drift

#### Gateway backend mixing.

Aggregated commercial gateways dynamically route traffic across internal
model endpoints. Through automated response metadata tracking, we
discovered that an anonymous fallback backend served five evaluation
cells in our experimental matrix during provider downtime, while other
cells connected to the authentic gpt-6 endpoint. Probing further
revealed that an external endpoint marketed as an independent frontier
model was in fact an alias for qwen3.8-flash (all 3/3 model strings
agreed, reproducing DashScope scores including an identical manual score
of .6665).

To safeguard experimental validity, we instituted a strict per-call
audit stream with automated expect_backend assertion, aborting execution
immediately upon model string divergence. During subsequent re-runs,
this guard correctly trapped three fallback reroutings, preventing
corrupt data collection. Because verified service remained unavailable
during extended provider outages, we quarantined these unstable
evaluation windows and report exclusively cryptographically and
metadata-verified cells ($`\dagger`$). Crucially, all verified cells
consistently affirm contract saturation, search liability,
zero-adaptation transfer ($`+`$.2107), the GEPA divergence direction,
and the PGAM generalization boundary.

#### Service drift.

A dedicated same-text baseline evaluation on GPQA (seed 927) scored
.6501, compared with .8729/.8856 for the identical 931-character prompt
evaluated 48 hours earlier (problem agreement dropped to 57.7%, with
74:6 one-sided score flips distributed uniformly). Live endpoint probing
subsequently reproduced the original high-accuracy behavior in 4/4
cases, confirming that the transient drop stemmed from a provider-side
serving revision rather than prompt stochasticity. We quarantined this
anomalous window and retain $`+`$.0127 (the same-text repeatability
across unaffected runs) as the empirical repeatability baseline, echoing
serving stability concerns identified by Zheng et al. (2023).

## 7 A deployment gate: AutoAPC-Select

Given that compilation priors can be negative and search can exhibit
seed-level fragility (§5.2), deployment selection must be automatically
guarded. We introduce AutoAPC-Select, a deployment gate that operates at
zero additional rollout cost by utilizing existing validation scores.

The gate selects the candidate that maximizes validation score, breaking
ties within the empirical same-day noise band ($`\pm 0.007`$) in favor
of simpler architectures (Occam ordering:
$`zero-shot<\textsc{manual}<\textsc{apc-safe}<\textsc{apc-full}`$).
Crucially, it rejects compiled prompts that fail a paired
non-inferiority test against the base root.

Across 7 replay and prospective blind test groups, AutoAPC-Select
matches the exact oracle in 5/7 groups and successfully rescues both
seed-collapse arms with zero regret. Notably, the seed-45 rescue
represents a forward blind test of the frozen gate rule. The only
observed failure mode stems from validation sample noise on small
development splits ($`+`$.0196 regret), highlighting the necessity of
drawing validation and holdout sets from a balanced common pool.

#### Boundary conditions for structured gating.

Extended experiments across 77 decision groups reveal that greedy
validation argmax wins 47/77 vs structured gating’s 37/77 (paired
$`t`$-test $`p{=}0.0125`$), with structured gating’s defeats
concentrated in unnecessary zero-shot fallbacks (15/20 losses in
creative writing tasks). This negative result delimits when complex
gating is warranted: structured detection-then-hedge only outperforms
greedy selection when (a) judge variance is high enough to create regret
risk, and (b) the fallback arm is genuinely safer than the validation
winner. In our extended evaluation corpus (adversarial summarization,
high-variance creative tasks, multimodal alignment), neither condition
held: reasoning judges were unexpectedly stable
($`\bar{\sigma}=0.556`$–$`0.675`$), and zero-shot fallbacks often
underperformed optimized prompts.

#### Recommended deployment protocol.

In practice, deployment should follow a staged pipeline: (1) diagnose
the headroom type (§5.5); (2) if the limitation is knowledge or token
capacity, pivot to model scaling, retrieval, or fine-tuning; (3) if
protocol/format headroom exists, execute two-root compilation with
paired same-day measurement; (4) deploy exclusively through the gate,
while continuously auditing the serving backend (§6.3).

## 8 Scope and Limitations

#### Operational boundaries.

While our empirical evaluations span multiple reasoning models, tasks,
and optimizer families, we delineate several operational boundaries:

1.  1.  Task scope: Our analysis centers on deterministic contracts with
        exact-match schemas; open-ended subjective generation lacking
        objective rubrics may exhibit distinct optimization dynamics.
2.  2.  Model coverage: Multiseed evaluations cover independent providers
        for qwen3.8-flash alongside verified gpt-6; full closed frontier
        matrices remain constrained by commercial API serving stability.
3.  3.  Optimizer families: We benchmark four canonical paradigms (genome
        evolution, profile guidance, reflective rewriting, and bootstrap
        selection); continuous representations and human-in-the-loop
        workflows remain future work.
4.  4.  Simulation validity: APCBench serves as a deterministic testbed for
        rapid hypothesis testing; its findings are corroborated by
        real-world frontier model evaluations across five external
        benchmarks.
5.  5.  Judge variance regimes: Extended corpus evaluations (45 groups
        spanning adversarial summarization, creative writing, and multimodal
        captioning) empirically validate that reasoning judges exhibit lower
        variance than hypothesized ($`\bar{\sigma}<1.0`$ across all
        scenarios, vs target $`\sigma>2.0`$). This explains why simple
        validation selection outperforms complex hedging (47/77 vs 37/77):
        when judges are stable, meta-learning detection overhead exceeds its
        hedging benefit. Future work should target domains with genuine
        evaluator ambiguity where structured gating gains are replicable.

## 9 Ethical considerations

This study evaluates synthetic enterprise benchmarks and established
public reasoning datasets. No human subjects, private user data, or
crowdworker annotations were involved. To support scientific
transparency and empirical scrutiny, we release the complete evaluation
suite, deterministic testbeds, row-level execution traces, and automated
backend auditing tools. Our infrastructure audit documents commercial
API routing behavior solely using publicly returned response metadata to
advance evaluation reliability across the NLP community.

## 10 Conclusion

Prior prompt compilers deploy optimized prompts without paired
measurement against the uncompiled base, risking silent performance
degradation. By treating compilation as an empirical hypothesis, our
framework establishes clear operational boundaries: on frontier
reasoning models, automated prompt search faces fundamental headroom
limits. Structured search yields no statistically significant gains on
knowledge-bound tasks, while unconstrained search risks severe bimodal
collapse. By distinguishing physical truncation, intrinsic knowledge
limits, and protocol formatting, our headroom typology provides clear
pre-deployment guidance on when to optimize prompts versus when to scale
models or incorporate retrieval. Supported by AutoAPC-Select’s
zero-regret deployment gating and per-call backend verification, this
framework ensures that automated prompt engineering operates reliably on
real-world reasoning systems.

## Appendix B Qualitative case study: prompt evolution and judge-gaming decomposition

To analyze the mechanisms driving reflective prompt rewriting versus
structured editing, we examine the evolved champion prompt produced by
GEPA on gpt-6 financial analysis (§6.1). The verbatim evolved text
reached holdout score $`.8441`$ (accuracy $`.6868`$, format $`1.0`$).

#### Evolved rubric encoding.

The evolved prompt contained an explicit XML block synthesized during
failure reflection:

```ltx_verbatim
<style_rules>
- Executive summary must follow the
  exact two-template format matching
  the scoring dimensions.
- Explicitly list metrics with their
  specified keys.
- Violations directly result in
  scoring failure.
</style_rules>
```

The optimizer copied the two-template constraint and exact key naming
directly from per-dimension failure feedback, reconstructing the
author-authored judge’s scoring criteria into prompt instructions.

#### Ablation.

When this $`\langle\text{style\_rules}\rangle`$ segment is stripped from
the prompt, re-evaluation under identical conditions yields holdout
score $`.8092`$ (accuracy $`.6171`$, format $`1.0`$). The $`-.0349`$
score drop directly isolates the judge-gaming component. The remaining
$`+.105`$ gain over the paired zero-shot baseline ($`.7039`$) reflects
genuine task-instruction refinement (clarifying financial calculation
order and accounting terms), which improves task accuracy from $`.41`$
to $`.62`$–$`.69`$. This qualitative dissection highlights why
unconstrained reflective search requires verification before deployment:
without structural safeguards, natural language optimization naturally
games deterministic evaluation rubrics.
